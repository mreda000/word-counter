Plan a Speech with a Word Budget and Rehearsal

Published by ChickenTask · Updated November 21, 2026

A speaking-time estimate gives you a starting point for a presentation, toast, or recorded script. Your actual delivery also includes pauses, emphasis, slide changes, demonstrations, and audience responses. Use the estimate to shape the draft, then rehearse the complete performance with a timer.

Understand the estimate on the page

The Word Counter & Text Analyzer assumes 130 words per minute for speaking and 200 words per minute for reading. These are fixed planning assumptions, not measurements of your voice or a recommended pace for every speaker.

Estimated speaking time in seconds = words ÷ 130 × 60

For comparison, the same 650-word draft has a reading estimate of 3 min 15 sec. Reading and speaking estimates answer different questions; neither includes time for a slide demonstration or questions.

Build a five-minute plan

Suppose your entire slot is five minutes, and you want to reserve one minute for pauses and showing an example. That leaves four minutes of planned speech. At the tool's assumed pace, the initial budget is 4 × 130 = 520 words.

  • Opening: about 65 words to state the purpose.
  • Main explanation: about 260 words for the central idea and evidence.
  • Spoken example: about 130 words, plus the reserved demonstration time.
  • Closing: about 65 words for the next step.

Those sections total 520 words. They are an example allocation, not a template you must follow. If questions belong inside the same five-minute slot, reserve time for them before calculating the draft budget.

Measure your own delivery

  1. Choose a representative passage and count its spoken words. Exclude stage directions you will not say.
  2. Read it aloud as you intend to present it, including natural pauses.
  3. Record the elapsed time in seconds.
  4. Calculate words × 60 ÷ elapsed seconds to estimate your effective words per minute.
  5. Rehearse the whole talk, including slides and demonstrations, because one passage may not represent every section.

For example, 260 words delivered in 150 seconds gives 104 words per minute. At that measured pace, four minutes of spoken material would be about 416 words, rather than the tool's 520-word estimate. ChickenTask does not currently provide a custom pace input; use this calculation alongside its fixed estimate.

Revise what costs time

If a rehearsal runs long, first check repeated context, a second example that makes the same point, or a conclusion that restates every detail. Protect the central answer and necessary evidence. Speaking faster can make an already dense explanation harder to follow, so test a shorter draft before relying on a faster delivery.

After editing, time another complete rehearsal and leave room for the unexpected. Use the worked editing examples to shorten sentences, and see the timing notes beside the tool when you need to check its assumptions.
