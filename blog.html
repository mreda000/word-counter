Practical writing help

Plan, edit, and measure your text.

These guides show how to use ChickenTask's Word Counter & Text Analyzer for a real task. Each explains a different decision: how to divide a word budget, what a result actually measures, which words to cut, or how much time a speech needs. The examples are included so you can check them in the tool.

Start with your task

You have a word limit

Plan the sections before trimming sentences. Check which parts count toward the limit, reserve room for your answer and evidence, and verify the finished document in the destination's own counter.

You have a draft that feels difficult to read

Find the unclear passage and identify what the reader needs to do. Use the statistics as clues, then check the actual meaning. A lower grade estimate or a smaller word total cannot judge accuracy for you.

You have a presentation deadline

Set aside time for pauses, slides, and questions before assigning a word budget. Rehearse aloud and measure your own pace; the tool's speaking estimate uses a fixed assumption.

Plan and edit

Use Word Counts to Plan an Essay or Brief

Compare two 600-word section plans and see how a draft can devote more room to its recommendation without becoming longer. Includes a check for what belongs in an assignment's limit.

5 Ways to Cut Words Without Losing Meaning

Turn a 28-word meeting notice into 18 words, then examine five editing moves and the information each must preserve. Includes a case where adding one word makes the message more useful.

Understand the numbers

Understand Your Word Counter Results

Check a two-line, nine-word example against expected results. Learn why abbreviations, line breaks, emoji, and decimal numbers can make counters disagree.

How to Interpret a Reading Level Estimate

See the calculation and its English-language limitations, then compare two sentences. Learn why a better score cannot tell you whether an instruction includes the information a reader needs.

Prepare and import

Plan a Speech with a Word Budget and Rehearsal

Build a five-minute plan that leaves time for a demonstration. Calculate an effective pace from a rehearsal and adjust the draft rather than relying only on a fixed estimate.

Analyze Webpage Text and Troubleshoot URL Fetching

Understand which text the importer reads, why a source page might refuse a browser request, and when copying a specific passage gives a more useful result.

Try one change, then check it

Keep your original draft in your own document. Paste a copy into the word counter, choose one question from the guides, and make a small revision. Compare the text as well as the numbers before keeping the edit. The tool does not check grammar, facts, or plagiarism.
